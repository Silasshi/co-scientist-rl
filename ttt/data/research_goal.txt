You are tasked with developing a method that allows pretrained LLMs to discover novel solutions to specific scientific problems at test time. Existing approaches with frozen LLMs cannot internalize new insights during search, while methods relying on large-scale execution feedback are infeasible for scientific domains where each evaluation is expensive. Your goal is to design a method where the LLM updates its own parameters during test-time search on a single target problem, learning from its own previous attempts. You must propose a methodology that interleaves search with learning, using only the problem's own reward signal as supervision. The method should operate within a fixed compute budget on open-weight models, and it should be evaluated by the quality of the single best solution found rather than the average.
